Conceptualizing students' understanding of local culture in integrated science and social studies (IPAS): A literature-based analysis

Authors

  • Nurul Sinta Nirwana Faculty of Education, Universitas Negeri Yogyakarta, Indonesia
  • Ratri Safitri Yosita Faculty of Education, Universitas Negeri Yogyakarta, Indonesia

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.21831/jpip.v19i2.97541

Keywords:

local cultural understanding, elementary education, IPAS learning, contextual learning, cultural literacy

Abstract

Although the integration of local culture into elementary education has gained traction, prior research has largely confined its focus to local culture as instructional media and teaching strategy. Limited attention has been given to how students actually develop awareness and comprehension of local culture within integrated learning processes. This study aims to analyze the concept of students' understanding of local culture in Ilmu Pengetahuan Alam dan Sosial (IPAS), an integrated Science and Social Studies subject, and to synthesize the literature into a conceptual perspective for culturally contextualized education. Employing a normative approach with a library research design, data were collected through systematic searches in Google Scholar, Scopus, and SINTA, yielding 14 relevant academic sources. Content analysis was conducted to identify recurring themes, conceptual relationships, and gaps. The synthesis suggests that students' understanding of local culture is a multidimensional construct encompassing cognitive, affective, behavioral, and social awareness dimensions. Based on the conceptual patterns identified, this research proposes a hierarchical conceptual model that describes the progression of local cultural understanding, from cultural exposure and experience toward deeper appreciation and value internalization. These findings should be interpreted as a conceptual synthesis rather than empirical evidence of student outcomes. The study contributes to the discourse on culturally contextualized education by establishing a conceptual foundation for integrating local culture into IPAS and providing a theoretical basis for future empirical investigations.
